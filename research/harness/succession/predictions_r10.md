# Predictions for succession r10 — closure trial 2, the second family

*2026-10-07. Root `d37cd91d…`, FROZEN and unchanged since trial 1
qualified. Written before any r10 room opens. The claude lineage's
first contact with this boundary since r3 — four bundles and roughly
fifteen pins ago.*

## Why this trial is different

Trial 1 (r9, codex) ran against a boundary hardened by five
consecutive codex generations: every seam it closed was a seam a codex
prior had exposed. The claude lineage has sampled none of that. It has
never met the verdict vocabulary pin, the sandbox signal, format-3
authoring, deep-audit transitivity, the jail floor and its uname
plank, producer freedom, the producer's HOME, the named target,
format 4, or either kill bound.

So this is the arc's first honest test of the era caveat in miniature:
is the boundary complete, or is it complete *for the prior that
sharpened it*? A different prior fills unpinned space differently —
that is the whole reason the bar demands two families.

## The predictions

- **P1. All twenty layers grow**, as both families have managed at
  every boundary to date.
- **P2. Identity passes.** The substituted full gate re-earns
  `REPO_OK` inside the hour. This is the prediction most at risk: it
  requires that fifteen pins written against codex divergences are
  enough to steer a different prior into the class.
- **P3. The recursive step passes** through the r10 CLI, and the
  bootstrap holds end to end (the producer-environment contract — jail,
  HOME, named target — is where a fresh prior is most likely to pick a
  different defensible default).
- **P4. Held-out tests**: 103/103.
- **P5. A clean matrix row** for `claude-r10`: correct verdicts, the
  shared fresh-authoring root (`cd117e55…`), its jail named.
- **P6. No halo.** The pair map against the original lands in the same
  band the codex trees occupy (449–485 divergent), rather than
  markedly higher — a family-level difference in *measured silence*
  would itself be a finding.

## The honest odds

Call P2 and P3 together a coin flip. Five codex generations found
3, 3, 3, 2, 1, 0 seams; a new family's first draw is precisely where
the next one should be expected, and the convergence reading does not
claim otherwise — it claims the *region* is drained for one prior.

## What each outcome means

- **Clean**: the count goes to **2 of 3**, and the "complete only for
  its own prior" worry is substantially answered. Trial 3 then needs
  one more clean run plus the outside-machine condition.
- **A seam**: the count stays at 1 unless the counterexample is
  ADOPTED — and adoption is a signature, not an automatic consequence.
  If it is signed, the count honestly returns to zero and the arc
  continues; that is the rule that makes the count mean anything. A
  seam found here is also the single most valuable finding available
  right now, because it would be the first evidence that the boundary
  is prior-specific.
