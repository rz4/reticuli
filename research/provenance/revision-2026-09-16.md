# Revision v2.2: the kernel claim moves `4b90feef…` → `e650b524…`

The kernel claim (`examples/kernel/`) was revised on 2026-09-16. Three
behaviors are newly pinned in `checks/kernel_check.py`, each measured before
it was demanded — the same discipline as the v2.1 revision, whose account is
`revision-2026-09-15.md`.

## The three pins

**1. No component of a declared path may be a symlink, and `..` is refused
outright.** Found as a live divergence between the two independent
implementations of `spec/identity.md`: a claim whose declared input was a
symlink to a file *inside* the claim sealed under the kernel and was refused
by the reference sealer — whether the claim existed depended on which
implementation looked at it. The deeper defect is aliasing: an internal link
from a pinned name to a generated output hashes implementation bytes into
the root at sealing, and materialization flattens the link, so a blind
rebuilder's room arrives holding the implementation it was never to see.
The hardlink rule (`st_nlink == 1`) had already closed this channel's twin.
Both implementations now refuse any symlink component; the suite pins the
internal-link case, the symlinked-directory case, the inside-but-through-`..`
case, and the honest nested control.

**2. Records are the crosscheck's second transport.** A record
(`spec/record.md`) is a signed statement of one machine's results, and
`crosscheck` accepts a record file as a leg wherever it accepts a claim
directory. The suite pins the reader (canonical bytes, closed vocabulary,
refusals in band, the `reticuli.record` namespace — three acts, three
namespaces, pairwise distinct), pins that frozen and live legs reach the
same verdict and mix freely, and pins the trust boundary: a proof lands on
M1's manifest so M1 must be a directory; directory legs are the caller's own
executions and stay anchor-free; record legs are relayed statements, and a
recorded proof refuses one whose signature the anchor does not verify, then
embeds each record's digest and signer identity.

**3. Audit of a self-referential claim reports completely.** The v2.1
crosscheck carried a dissent: one of three independent kernels failed its
`audit` on the kernel claim alone — a claim whose gate is itself a kernel,
running nested — and reported no gate name, no sandbox, no detail. The suite
had only ever exercised `audit` against small fixtures (finding 12). It now
audits a claim whose generated output is a kernel and whose gate seals,
verifies, and audits an inner claim with it, and requires the report to
carry the gate's name, a status from the vocabulary, and a recorded sandbox,
never null.

## Re-earning the proof

A revision orphans its predecessor's crosscheck by design. What re-earning
costs, from this project's own ledger: the v2.1 proof was one blind `gpt-5`
rebuild against the 1,351-line suite — 895 lines, 12 minutes, 603,930
tokens, $3.40, passing both sandbox environments on the first attempt — after
an earlier three-round search cost $18.50. The revised suite is larger (the
record reader and the nested-audit fixture), so expect the same order of
magnitude, with the variance living in how many holes the new pins turn out
to have: every failed round is the suite being measured, and is paid for
again.

The shape of the run, as before: a producer that has never seen this
repository's kernel, in a room holding only the recipe and the suite, under
the environment matrix (both sandbox backends); then the three-machine test
with the original, a byte copy, and the rebuild; then `record_proof` on a
pass. Independence remains a declaration either way.
