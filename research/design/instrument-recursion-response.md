# Response — the instrument-recursion proposal, and what we adopt first

*2026-10-07. The keyholder supplied a full technical design report
("Recursive improvement of Reticuli through reconstructible discovery
instruments", baselined at commit fdd3e81, archived beside this note
as instrument-recursion.md). This is the project's assessment and the
adoption order. Not normative; nothing here moves a root.*

## Assessment in brief

The report names the correct next recursion: the discovery
instruments — silence maps, probe generators, reducers, judges,
schedulers — as versioned, contracted, reconstructible, COMPETED
artifacts, with replayable witnesses and an adjudication layer
separating "a question" (divergence) from "a finding" (an
independently justified obligation, failed by a gate-passing member).
Three loops kept distinct: subject refinement, instrument improvement,
and reconstruction of investigative capability.

Its sharpest observation about this repository: §7's hypothesis —
paired production/consumption branches tested separately, never
together — IS the seam species found five consecutive times here
(generated/inputs globs, copy/rewrite room paths, the producer-env
planks, the vocabulary halves, the format-era splits). The report
generalized our dominant failure class into an instrument before we
did. Its required negative control (valid alternative representations
stay accepted) is the mistake this project made twice in one week
(the kill bound, the canonical-order assertion), stated as design
rather than learned per incident. And its replay/schema discipline
would have structurally caught both instrument misfires of the same
week (a stale diagnostic path; a vacuous all() over an empty set) that
were instead caught by vigilance.

## Corrections to it

- `closure_check` already exists and is PINNED — the report lists its
  first example instrument as future work.
- Instrument claims are structurally weaker than subject claims, by
  design: effectiveness cannot be pinned without memorizing fixtures,
  so the third loop rests on evaluation distributions OUTSIDE the
  claims. Different trust architecture; say so loudly.
- The scheduler loop (§9) is premature with one subject family.
- It misses the synthesis available here: its transfer targets
  (serializers, converters, migrations) describe jq exactly. The
  first instrument campaign and the jq arm are one experiment, not
  two priorities.

## Adoption order

1. **Finish the closure arc** (the k=3 set in progress). No new
   machinery until the first-order question is answered.
2. **Build ONE instrument from ONE failure class**: the
   symmetric-pair / round-trip detector of §7, with the minimal
   witness record (§6's fields, content-addressed), developed against
   the five historical seams as fixtures plus injected defects and
   valid-variant negative controls. Wrap, do not rewrite, the
   existing silence/closure/crossjudge tools as the baseline (§14's
   adapter rule).
3. **Its first transfer target is jq**, merging this campaign with
   the jq preregistration (research/design/
   jq-negative-space-experiment.md): the instrument hunts
   producer-consumer gaps in a subject its authors did not write,
   scored against the gojq/jaq divergence ground truth.
4. Defer: the evidence cache, composition graph, learned scheduler,
   and full fourteen-module architecture — roadmap, revisited after
   (2) and (3) produce or fail to produce a transfer result.

The report's §20 claim is the right first milestone and is adopted
verbatim as the instrument campaign's success criterion: one
discovered improvement to the investigative machinery, expressed in a
claim well enough to reconstruct independently, retaining a measured
advantage on fresh targets.
